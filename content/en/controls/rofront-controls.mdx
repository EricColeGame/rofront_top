If you searched for "RoFront controls" expecting a hotkey sheet, a command list, or a tidy diagram of every button, the honest answer is that no such document exists in the public record. RoFront [World PvP] is a real-time territory conquest game on Roblox by the creator Torrey Pines, and its official description covers what you can do — expand, attack neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances, launch nukes — without ever naming a single input. That gap is not a flaw in your search. It is the actual state of the documentation, and knowing it saves you from trusting a fabricated control list.

This article separates what the developer advertises from what a third-party guide observed, and from what remains undocumented. Everything below is grounded in the official Roblox listing for [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) and one independent guide, attributed where used.

## What the official listing actually says about controls

The verified description reads, in full: "Based on the Open Front game. Real-time territory conquest. Expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances and launch nukes against 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes. Conquer 80% of the world to win. Map Credits to Open Front."

That is the authority on game identity and advertised features. Notice what it does not contain: no keybinds, no mouse instructions, no touch gestures, no chat commands, no codes, no settings menu walkthrough. The description names verbs — expand, attack, build, forge, launch — but never the physical inputs that trigger them.

This matters because Roblox experiences vary widely. Some use click-to-select, some use drag, some use keyboard shortcuts, some use radial menus, and some use a combination that changes between devices. None of that can be assumed for RoFront from the listing alone. Open Front's own rules and controls cannot establish RoFront's either; the listing credits Open Front for the map, not for the interface.

## What a third-party guide observed

An independent guide at [Nisu](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/) offers the closest thing to a control description currently available, and it is explicitly framed as observation rather than documentation. The guide states that its author had not played a live RoFront match, and it draws interface details from a player video by APEX ROBLOX published October 10, 2026.

According to that guide's reading of the footage, the screen shows the world-control goal at the top, colored borders across the map, and "the player's troop, gold and attack controls below." That is a description of visible interface regions, not a keybind list. The guide recommends pausing on the interface and matching one visible control to the action that follows, which is sound advice precisely because no authoritative mapping exists.

Treat this as a secondary observation. It tells you roughly where to look on screen. It does not tell you which key or tap does what, and it should not be repeated as if it were developer documentation.

## How to verify RoFront controls yourself

Since the sources do not document inputs, verification has to happen in the experience. A practical routine:

1. Open the official page and confirm the creator reads Torrey Pines before joining. Similar names can lead to unrelated games.
2. On your first match, locate your territory and its borders before touching anything. Identify who shares your boundary.
3. Find the world-control display rather than judging progress by how large your colored area looks at the current zoom.
4. Make one deliberate move. Watch what changes — territory gained, nation information, any shift along another border.
5. Repeat with a single change at a time so you can attribute cause to input.

This is a learning method, not a measured mechanic. It is advice inferred from a territory conquest premise: in any game where borders and resources shift in real time, isolating one variable per attempt is the fastest way to build a reliable mental model.

## Buildings, alliances, and nukes as control problems

Each advertised system implies its own set of inputs, and none are documented. The listing names cities, ports and factories as buildable, but provides no economy table, so it cannot establish a cheapest route or a universal build order. When you inspect a building option, read its displayed cost and intended benefit, compare it with your current position, and watch the result. Treat the three as separate options to learn rather than buying whichever icon a video shows first.

Alliances and nuclear attacks carry relationship consequences, so read the relevant in-game controls before acting. A dramatic strike in a recording does not establish that rushing toward nukes is the right opening for your position. This is strategic reasoning from a conquest premise, not a measured claim about balance.

## The 88-nation figure and server size

The description mentions 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes. Separately, the Roblox Server Size field showed 30 when the Nisu guide checked on October 11, 2026. These are different fields, and the listing does not explain how they relate. Do not treat 88 as tested simultaneous-player capacity. Use current Roblox server information when choosing where to join.
