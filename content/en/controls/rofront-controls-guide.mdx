RoFront [World PvP] is a Roblox strategy experience by Torrey Pines built around real-time territory conquest. According to the official game page, you expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes while competing against other nations and tribes. The published victory condition is to conquer 80% of the world.

That description tells you what the game is about. It does not tell you which button does what. This RoFront controls guide is written around that gap: what the interface is documented to show, how to learn the controls yourself in a live match, and why you should be skeptical of any "full controls list" that does not come from the game or the creator.

## What the official description actually confirms

The verified listing for [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) confirms the following as advertised features:

- Real-time territory conquest
- Expansion from a single starting nation
- Attacking neighboring nations
- Building cities, ports and factories
- Forging alliances
- Launching nuclear attacks
- A stated win condition of controlling 80% of the world
- A map credited to Open Front

Notice what is missing. There is no published keybind list, no documented hotkeys, no chat command reference, and no developer explanation of how each control is bound on PC, mobile, or console. The description names systems, not inputs. Any guide that presents a tidy list of RoFront hotkeys without showing where it came from is guessing.

## Why a controls guide has to start with the interface

A third-party guide from Nisu, which describes itself as an independent, unaffiliated resource, offers a useful observation: in a sampled RoFront match video, the screen shows the world-control goal at the top, colored borders across the map, and the player's troop, gold and attack controls below. That is a description of layout, not a keybind chart, and the guide is careful to frame it that way.

This matters for controls specifically. In a map-based conquest game, most of your inputs are probably contextual: you select something on the map, then act on it. That means the fastest way to learn RoFront controls is not memorizing a list. It is watching which on-screen element changes when you click, tap, or press something.

## A practical method for learning the controls in your first match

The following is strategy advice inferred from a territory conquest premise, not measured mechanics. Treat it as a routine, not a rulebook.

1. **Open the experience through the official page.** Use the [Torrey Pines listing](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) and confirm the creator name before joining. Searches for similar names can surface unrelated games.
2. **Locate your territory and its borders first.** Before touching anything, identify who shares your boundary. The advertised loop includes attacking neighbors, so knowing your neighbors is the prerequisite for any attack input.
3. **Find the world-control display.** The Nisu guide recommends judging progress by this meter rather than by how large your colored area looks at your current zoom level. That is sound advice for a conquest game with an 80% goal.
4. **Make one deliberate move, then stop.** Watch what changed: your territory, your displayed resources, and any border elsewhere on the map. One action at a time gives you a clean cause-and-effect reference.
5. **Match one visible control to one action.** If you are watching footage, pause on the interface and identify a single control, then resume long enough to see the result. Copying a number from a single frame teaches you nothing about your own position.

| What you want to learn | Where to look | What not to assume |
|---|---|---|
| Movement or map navigation | On-screen elements in your own match | That a video's camera behavior is a keybind |
| Attacking a neighbor | The controls shown near your nation info | That a fixed attack percentage always wins |
| Building cities, ports, factories | The build options and their displayed costs | That one build order is universally optimal |
| Alliances and nukes | The relevant in-game controls | That a dramatic strike is a good opening |

## Buildings, alliances and nukes: controls you should read before using

The official description names cities, ports and factories as buildable, and alliances and nuclear attacks as available actions. It does not publish a cost table, an economy formula, or a requirements list. The Nisu guide makes the same point from the outside: the listing cannot establish a universal cheapest route to victory, so inspect the current options and their displayed costs before committing.

For alliances and nukes specifically, the sensible habit is to read the in-game control before acting, because both can change your relationships with other nations. A nuclear strike that looks decisive in a recording does not establish that rushing toward it is the right opening for your position.

## The 88-nation figure and server size are different things

The official description refers to 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes. That is a claim about the game world as described by the creator. It is not the same field as Roblox's Server Size, which the Nisu guide reports showed 30 when checked on October 11, 2026. Those are separate values, and the listing does not explain how they relate. Do not treat 88 as a tested simultaneous-player capacity, and do not treat a page update timestamp as a gameplay patch.

## What is not documented, and how to verify it yourself

As of this writing, there is no developer-published controls reference for RoFront. No official hotkey list, no mobile gesture documentation, no console mapping, and no creator-posted control chart. That is a documentation gap, not a hidden feature set.

To verify controls reliably:

- Join through the [official RoFront page](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) and check the creator is Torrey Pines.
- Test one input at a time and observe the result before repeating it.
- Check the current Roblox server information when choosing where to join.
- Treat third-party guides, including the [Nisu RoFront guide](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/), as secondary observations rather than developer documentation.
