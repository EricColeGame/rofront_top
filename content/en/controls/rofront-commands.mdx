If you searched for "RoFront commands," you probably expected a list of chat codes, hotkeys, or console-style inputs. Here is the honest starting point: the official Roblox listing for RoFront [World PvP] does not publish a command list. What it does publish is a set of gameplay systems — territory expansion, attacking neighbors, building cities, ports and factories, forging alliances, and launching nukes — plus a stated victory condition of conquering 80% of the world. That gap between what players search for and what is documented is worth understanding before you spend a match typing guesses into a chat box.

## What the official description actually documents

The verified description from creator Torrey Pines describes RoFront as "real-time territory conquest" based on the Open Front game, with map credits to Open Front. It lists these advertised features:

- Expanding from a single nation
- Attacking your neighbors
- Building cities, ports and factories
- Forging alliances
- Launching nukes
- Competing against "88 player nations and hundreds of tribes"
- Winning by conquering 80% of the world

That is the complete advertised feature set. Notice what is missing: no mention of a command console, no slash commands, no keybind list, no chat syntax. The description tells you what you can do in the game, not how you input it.

One important clarification: the "88 player nations" figure comes from the description text. It is not a verified server capacity. Roblox lists server size as a separate field on the game page, and that number can differ from the description's nation count. Treat 88 as a design claim about the world, not a promise about how many players share your server.

## Why "commands" is a misleading search term here

RoFront is a map-based strategy game, not a soldier-level shooter and not a console-driven sandbox. The third-party Nisu guide makes this point directly: a tutorial for an unrelated Roblox shooter will not explain this experience, and searches for similar names can lead to unrelated games or videos.

In territory conquest games, your "commands" are usually interface actions — clicking a border to push into it, selecting a building option, confirming a diplomatic action. Those are controls, not typed commands. If RoFront has a chat command layer, it is not documented in any source available here, and inventing one would be irresponsible.

## Reading the interface instead of hunting for codes

If you are coming from games where commands unlock advantages, reset your expectations. The advertised systems in RoFront are strategic, not input-based:

- **Expansion** happens through border interaction, and the Nisu guide advises watching your borders and the world-control meter before committing to an attack.
- **Buildings** — cities, ports and factories — are presented as separate options to learn. The official listing does not provide an economy table or a universal cheapest build order, so any "best first build" claim you see elsewhere is unverified.
- **Alliances** involve other nations. The Nisu guide recommends reading the relevant in-game controls before acting, especially when an action changes relationships.
- **Nukes** are advertised, but requirements and consequences are not documented publicly. A dramatic strike in a video does not establish that rushing toward nukes is a good opening.

Strategic advice here is inferred from the territory conquest premise, not measured from game data. The logic is simple: in a game where you win at 80% world control, every action should move that meter or protect your ability to move it later.
