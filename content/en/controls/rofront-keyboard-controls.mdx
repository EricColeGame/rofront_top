If you searched for "RoFront keyboard controls" hoping for a tidy hotkey list, the honest answer is that no such list exists in any source tied to the game. The official Roblox page for [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) by Torrey Pines describes the game's premise and features, but it does not publish a control scheme. That gap matters, because RoFront is a real-time territory conquest game, not a soldier-level shooter, and its inputs are likely tied to map actions rather than movement and aiming. This article separates what is actually documented from what players must verify themselves.

## What the Official Description Actually Confirms

The verified listing describes RoFront as "Based on the Open Front game. Real-time territory conquest." It says you "Expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances and launch nukes against 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes," and that you "Conquer 80% of the world to win." Map credits go to Open Front.

That is the full extent of the authoritative material. Notice what is absent: no key bindings, no mouse instructions, no mention of camera controls, no chat commands, no codes, and no settings menu documentation. The description tells you what you can do in the world, not which button does it.

This distinction is worth internalizing. A feature appearing in the description — attacking, building, allying, launching nukes — does not mean a specific key is documented for it. Anyone claiming a definitive RoFront hotkey list is either reading it off their own screen or guessing.

## Why "Keyboard Controls" Is the Wrong Mental Model

RoFront inherits its premise from Open Front, a map-based strategy game. In that genre, you typically interact with territory, borders, and build menus rather than piloting a character. The Nisu guide, an independent third-party resource, describes RoFront as "a map-based strategy game" and explicitly warns that "a tutorial for the Roblox shooter Frontlines will not explain this experience."

That warning is the most useful thing a controls-focused reader can take away. If you arrive expecting WASD movement, sprint, crouch, or weapon switching, you are importing assumptions from first-person shooters that the sources do not support for RoFront. The advertised verbs are territorial: expand, attack, build, ally, nuke. Controls, whatever they are, serve those verbs.

## What a Third-Party Guide Observes About the Interface

The Nisu guide is secondary observation, not developer documentation, but it offers one concrete interface detail. Reviewing a player video from APEX ROBLOX, it notes that "the screen shows the world-control goal at the top, colored borders across the map and the player's troop, gold and attack controls below."

That tells you the interface surfaces troop, gold, and attack controls in a lower panel — plausibly clickable or keyboard-adjacent elements rather than hidden commands. It does not tell you which keys map to them. The guide also cautions that the sampled video runs at a visible 2× speed marker, so you should not read its playback timing as real-time income or attack speed. The same caution applies to controls: a fast video can make an input look instantaneous when it is not.

## How to Verify Controls Yourself in the Actual Experience

Since no source documents the bindings, verification has to happen in-game. A practical routine:

1. Open the official experience through the [RoFront page](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) and confirm the creator reads Torrey Pines. Similar names can lead to unrelated games.
2. Before acting, locate your territory, its borders, and the neighboring nations. The Nisu guide suggests this as a first-match step.
3. Make one deliberate move — a single expansion or attack — and watch what changes: territory gained, information shown for your nation, and any shift along another border.
4. Check whether Roblox's own settings menu exposes input options. Standard Roblox client settings are separate from any game-specific bindings and may not reflect them.
5. If a control is unclear, ask in the experience itself rather than trusting a search result.
