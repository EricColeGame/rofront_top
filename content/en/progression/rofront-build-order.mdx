If you search for a RoFront build order, you probably want a clean sequence: build X, then Y, then Z, and win. That sequence does not exist in any documented source. The official Roblox listing for [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) by Torrey Pines describes a real-time territory conquest game based on Open Front, where you expand from a single nation, attack neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes. It also states the victory condition: conquer 80% of the world.

What the listing does not provide is a cost table, an income formula, or a recommended purchase order. So this guide treats "build order" as a decision framework rather than a script. Everything below separates what is advertised, what is reasonable strategy inference, and what you must verify inside the actual experience.

## What the official description actually confirms

The verified description is short and specific. It confirms these systems exist:

- Territory expansion from one starting nation
- Attacks on neighboring nations
- Cities, ports, and factories as buildable structures
- Alliances between nations
- Nuclear attacks
- A win condition of 80% world control
- A world populated by "88 player nations and hundreds of tribes," per the description

That last figure is worth reading carefully. The description mentions 88 player nations, but that is a description claim about the game world, not a confirmed server capacity. A third-party guide from [Nisu](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/) noted that Roblox's separate Server Size field showed 30 when checked, and correctly warned against equating the two numbers. Treat 88 as flavor from the listing, and check the live server info when you join.

## Why no universal build order is documented

A build order implies fixed costs, fixed build times, and a known economy. None of that appears in the official description. The Nisu guide, which is a third-party observation and not developer documentation, explicitly says the listing "does not provide a complete economy table" and cannot establish a universal cheapest route to victory. It also recommends inspecting each building's displayed cost and intended benefit before buying.

That is the honest state of things. Anyone presenting a rigid RoFront build order with exact timings is either working from undocumented data or guessing. The safe approach is to build your own order through observation.

## A reasoning framework for your first builds

The following is strategy inference from a territory conquest premise, not measured mechanics. Use it as a checklist, not a recipe.

**1. Read your position before spending.** Locate your territory, your borders, and your neighbors. The official loop includes attacking neighbors, so knowing who shares your boundary matters more than any opening purchase.

**2. Watch the world-control meter.** The description sets an 80% goal. A large colored area at your current zoom is not the same as progress toward that number. Check the actual display.

**3. Ask what each building is for.** Cities, ports, and factories are named as separate options. Before choosing one, read its in-game description and cost, then compare it to your current situation. If you are boxed in by neighbors, a structure that supports expansion may matter more than one that supports something else. If you have room to grow, the calculus changes.

**4. Make one deliberate move, then observe.** The Nisu guide suggests making a single move and watching what changes: territory gained, nation information, and any shift along another border. This builds a personal reference for the next decision.

**5. Treat alliances and nukes as relationship decisions.** Both are advertised features. Both can change how other nations treat you. Read the relevant controls before acting, especially for anything with visible consequences.

| Advertised feature | What to verify in your match |
|---|---|
| Territory expansion | Your borders, your target, world-control progress |
| Cities, ports, factories | Displayed cost and stated benefit for each |
| Alliances | Who is involved and what the controls offer |
| Nuclear attacks | Requirements and consequences before confirming |

## Using video footage without copying it blindly

Match footage can teach the interface faster than text. A player video referenced by the Nisu guide shows the world-control goal at the top of the screen, colored borders, and troop, gold, and attack controls below. That is useful for learning where to look.

It is not useful as a build order source. The same guide notes the sampled segment runs at 2× speed, so playback timing tells you nothing about real income or attack speed. A single frame also cannot tell you whether a purchase was optimal for that player's position, let alone yours.

## What to do when you cannot find an answer

If you want to know whether a specific build order exists, whether custom maps are supported, or whether there are codes or community links, check the source of truth: the [official RoFront experience page](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) and the creator, Torrey Pines. If a feature is not in the description and not visible in the game's own interface, it is not documented. Do not trust a number pulled from a video frame or a guide that presents guesses as facts.
