RoFront [World PvP] is a real-time territory conquest game on Roblox from the creator Torrey Pines. Its official description states that it is based on the Open Front game, that you expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports, and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes, and that you win by conquering 80% of the world. The same description credits Open Front for the map.

## What "expansion" means in RoFront

Expansion in RoFront is territorial. You begin with one nation, and the map is divided among other nations and tribes. Growth means taking and holding more of that map, not leveling a character or unlocking a class.

The official description frames the loop as: expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, and build. It also names a concrete finish line: control 80% of the world. That number is the published victory condition, so treat it as the target the match is measured against rather than a milestone you pass on the way to something else.

One important caveat: the description mentions "88 player nations and hundreds of tribes." That is a description of the game world, not a confirmed server capacity. Roblox lists server size as a separate field, and the two numbers should not be treated as the same thing. Check the current server information on the [official RoFront page](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) before you join if player count matters to you.

## Reading the map before you commit

Because expansion is territorial, the most useful early habit is reading your position rather than acting immediately. A third-party guide from Nisu, which describes itself as an independent, unplayed guide, suggests locating your territory, its borders, and your neighboring nations first, and watching the world-control display instead of judging progress by how large your colored area looks at the current zoom.

That advice is reasonable for any map-based conquest game, and it is explicitly framed as a suggestion rather than measured mechanics. The practical version:

- Identify who shares your border before you move.
- Check the world-control meter, not just your own territory size.
- Make one deliberate move, then watch what changes on the map and in your nation's displayed information.
- If a push goes badly, ask which decision you would change, rather than assuming a fixed percentage always wins.

None of this requires knowing hidden formulas. It requires paying attention to what the interface already shows you.

A defensible approach is to treat each building as a separate option to learn. Before you build, ask what you are trying to improve with this choice, read whatever the game displays for that building, and then watch the result. ## Alliances and nukes: read before you act

Alliances and nuclear attacks are both named in the official description, which means they are part of the advertised feature set. It does not mean they are simple, cheap, or safe.

Diplomacy in a territory game usually changes who can attack you and who will help when someone else does. A nuclear option usually carries consequences beyond the immediate target. Because RoFront's listing does not explain either system, the honest advice is procedural: open the relevant in-game controls, read what they say, and consider how the action changes your relationships with other nations before confirming. A dramatic strike in someone's video does not establish that rushing toward it is a good opening from your position.
