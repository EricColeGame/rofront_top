A RoFront battle is not a firefight. It is a map-wide contest over borders, resources, and the world-control meter. RoFront [World PvP] is a Roblox strategy experience by Torrey Pines, built on the Open Front game concept and credited to Open Front for its map. You start with a single nation, expand outward, and compete against other nations and tribes. The published victory condition is simple to state and hard to reach: conquer 80% of the world.

If you searched for RoFront battle tips expecting loadouts or weapon stats, this is a different genre. There are no classes, rifles, or kill/death ratios here. The relevant decisions are about where your borders sit, who your neighbors are, what you build, and when an alliance or a nuclear strike changes the board. This guide separates what the official listing actually advertises from strategy that follows logically from a territory conquest premise, and it flags what remains undocumented.

## What the Official Description Actually Says

According to the [official RoFront [World PvP] page](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront), the game is real-time territory conquest based on Open Front. The advertised features are:

- Expansion from a single nation
- Attacking neighboring nations
- Building cities, ports, and factories
- Forging alliances
- Launching nukes
- Competing against "88 player nations and hundreds of tribes"
- Winning by conquering 80% of the world

That is the full published feature set. The listing does not include costs, build times, income formulas, troop types, damage values, hotkeys, or balance numbers. Any guide that supplies those specifics without citing the game itself is guessing. Treat the description as the authority on identity and advertised features, and treat everything else as observation or inference.

## Reading the Board Before You Commit

A RoFront battle begins with information, not aggression. Before you attack anyone, locate your territory, identify which nations share your borders, and find the world-control display. The official loop includes attacking neighbors, so knowing exactly who borders you is the starting point for any deliberate move.

One practical habit: make a single move, then watch what changes. Note the territory you gained, the information shown for your nation, and whether any other border shifted. That gives you a reference point for the next decision without relying on an unverified opening build order.

The 80% goal matters here. A successful local attack improves your position, but it does not win the match. Progress is measured against the world-control meter, not against how large your colored area looks at your current zoom level. Judging progress by map appearance alone is a common way to misread a RoFront battle.

## Cities, Ports, and Factories: Advertised, Not Explained

The description names cities, ports, and factories as buildable. It does not explain what each one does, what they cost, or how they interact. That gap is important. A third-party guide from [Nisu](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/) notes that the listing provides no complete economy table and therefore cannot establish a universal cheapest route to victory. That is a fair reading.

The useful approach is to inspect each option in-game and ask what you are trying to improve with the choice. Read the information shown for the building, compare it with your current situation, and watch the result. Treat the three as separate systems to learn rather than buying whichever icon appears first in a video.

| Advertised feature | What to check in your match |
|---|---|
| Territory expansion | Your borders, your target, world-control progress |
| Cities, ports, factories | Current description, displayed cost, intended benefit |
| Alliances | Who is involved, what the current controls offer |
| Nuclear attacks | Requirements and consequences of the selected action |

## Alliances and Nukes Change Relationships

Alliances and nuclear attacks are both part of the published feature set, and both are relationship-changing actions. The description does not explain how alliances are formed, broken, or enforced, and it does not describe nuclear requirements or blast effects. Because of that, read the relevant in-game controls before acting, especially when an action affects other nations.

A dramatic nuclear strike in a gameplay video does not establish that rushing toward nukes is the best opening for every position. It shows one player's decision in one match. Strategy here is inference from a territory conquest premise, not measured mechanics: in most map-control games, an action that alters your relationships carries diplomatic cost alongside any territorial gain. Verify that assumption inside RoFront rather than importing it from another game.

## The 88-Nation Claim and Server Size

The description mentions 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes. That is a description claim, not a verified server capacity. On the same official Roblox page, the separate Server Size field showed 30 when a third-party guide checked it on October 11, 2026. These are different fields, and the listing does not explain how the two counts relate.

Use the current Roblox server information when choosing where to join. Do not assume an 88-player lobby, and do not treat a crowd size in a recording as proof of performance on your device. For a first session, focus on a few decisions: recognize your position, make a measured expansion, inspect one building, and follow the control meter toward the stated 80% goal.

## Learning From Footage Without Copying It

Match footage is useful for learning the interface, not for copying numbers. A player video referenced by the Nisu guide shows the world-control goal at the top, colored borders across the map, and troop, gold, and attack controls below. Watch where the player looks before each action, pause on the interface, and match one visible control to the result that follows.

Be careful with playback speed. If a recording shows a speed marker, its timing is not a real-time measurement of income or attack speed. It is a demonstration of one match, not proof of an optimal strategy or the outcome you will get.

## FAQ

**How do you win a RoFront battle?**
The official Torrey Pines description says to conquer 80% of the world. Follow the world-control display in the match rather than assuming one large territory or one attack has won the game.

**Does RoFront support 88 players in one server?**
The description mentions 88 player nations, but Roblox's separate Server Size field showed 30 when checked. These are different fields, and live capacity has not been verified.

**What should I build first?**
Inspect the current city, port, and factory options and choose according to your position and their displayed requirements. The official listing does not establish a universal optimal buying order.

**Where are the controls, custom maps, or community links documented?**
They are not documented in the sources reviewed here. Open the [RoFront experience on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront), confirm the creator is Torrey Pines, and read the in-game controls directly. Searches for similar names can lead to unrelated games, including shooters that share none of these systems.
