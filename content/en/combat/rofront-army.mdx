In RoFront [World PvP], the "army" is not a squad of soldiers you steer through trenches. It is the sum of everything you command as a nation: your borders, your expansion pushes, your cities, ports, factories, alliances, and the world-control progress that decides the match. If you searched for a RoFront army expecting classes, loadouts, or first-person combat, this guide will reset that expectation and explain what the game actually asks you to manage.

RoFront is a Roblox strategy experience by Torrey Pines, playable at [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront). The official description calls it real-time territory conquest based on Open Front, and it credits Open Front for the map. You begin with a single nation, expand outward, attack neighbors, and compete for control of the world.

## What the RoFront Army Actually Is

The verified description lists the core pieces of your command: expanding from one nation, attacking neighbors, building cities, ports, and factories, forging alliances, and launching nukes. It also states the win condition plainly: conquer 80% of the world.

That framing matters for anyone searching "RoFront army." There is no documented roster of troop types, no published damage values, and no official breakdown of how combat resolves. The description does not describe unit classes, equipment, or a first-person shooting layer. Treating RoFront like a shooter will send you looking for mechanics the official listing never advertises.

What you can rely on is the territory premise. Your "army" is your nation's reach across the map, and the world-control meter is the honest scoreboard. A large colored blob at one zoom level is not proof you are winning; the control display is the number the game tells you to watch.

## Reading Your Position Before You Expand

The official loop starts with attacking neighbors, so the first practical step is identifying who shares your border. Before committing to any push, locate your territory, note the nations touching it, and check the world-control display.

A sensible learning routine, inferred from the conquest premise rather than from measured mechanics, is to make one deliberate move and observe what changes. Did your territory grow? Did another border shift? Did the information panel for your nation update? That single-move habit builds a reference you can reuse, and it beats copying an opening build order from a video whose resources and neighbors differ from yours.

If a push goes badly, the useful question is which decision to change next time: the target, the timing, or a threat you ignored. Those are judgment calls you investigate in your own match.

## Alliances and Nukes Change Relationships

Alliances and nuclear attacks are part of the published feature set, and both can alter how other nations treat you. The description confirms they exist; it does not explain their requirements or effects. Before acting, read the relevant in-game controls, especially for anything that changes relationships.

A dramatic nuclear strike in a recording does not establish that rushing toward nukes is the best opening for every position. That is a strategic suggestion at most, and the official listing does not support it as a rule.

## The 88-Nation Claim and Server Size

The description refers to 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes. That is an advertised figure, not a tested simultaneous-player capacity. On the official Roblox page, the separate Server Size field showed 30 when a third-party guide checked it on October 11, 2026. Those are different fields, and the listing does not explain how they relate.

Use the current Roblox server information when choosing where to join. Neither the map description nor crowd size in a recording establishes performance on your device.
