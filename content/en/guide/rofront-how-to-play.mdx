## What RoFront Actually Is

According to the official Roblox listing, RoFront is "based on the Open Front game" and is a "real-time territory conquest" experience. You expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes. The description also states you compete against 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes, and that you win by conquering 80% of the world. Map credits go to Open Front.

Two clarifications matter before you play. First, RoFront is not a soldier-level first-person shooter. There are no classes, rifles, or kill/death ratios to manage. You are directing a nation on a map, not a single character. Second, the "88 player nations" figure comes from the description. Roblox also lists a separate Server Size field, and a third-party guide from [Nisu](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/) noted that field showed 30 when checked. Treat 88 as an advertised world figure, not a confirmed simultaneous-player capacity.

## Reading the Map Before You Attack

The official description confirms the core loop: expand, attack neighbors, and push toward 80% world control. What it does not document is a specific opening build order, so any advice here is strategic reasoning from the territory-conquest premise, not measured mechanics.

A sensible first move is reconnaissance. Locate your starting territory, identify which nations share your borders, and find the world-control display rather than judging progress by how large your colored area looks at the current zoom. Because attacking neighbors is part of the advertised loop, knowing who borders you is more useful than memorizing a number.

The third-party Nisu guide suggests a learning routine worth borrowing: make one deliberate move, watch what changes, then repeat. If a push goes poorly, ask whether the target was poorly chosen, whether you ignored a neighboring threat, or whether you acted without understanding the current controls. Those questions are more productive than copying a percentage from a video.

## Buildings, Alliances, and Nukes

The description names cities, ports and factories as buildable structures, plus alliances and nuclear attacks. It does not publish costs, income formulas, or a universal "cheapest route to victory." That means you should inspect each option in-game and ask what you are trying to improve before committing.

| Advertised feature | What to check in your match |
| --- | --- |
| Territory expansion | Your borders, your target, world-control progress |
| Cities, ports, factories | Current description, displayed cost, intended benefit |
| Alliances | Who is involved and what the controls offer |
| Nuclear attacks | Requirements and consequences of the action |

Treat each building as a separate system to learn rather than buying whichever icon a video shows first. Alliances and nukes can change relationships with other nations, so read the relevant controls before acting. A dramatic strike in a clip does not prove that rushing toward nukes is the best opening for your position.

## The 80% Goal and How to Track It

The published victory condition is conquering 80% of the world. Keep that goal visible while learning everything else. A successful local attack can improve your position without completing the match, so follow the world-control meter rather than assuming one large territory has won the game.

## FAQ

The official Torrey Pines description says to conquer 80% of the world. Track the world-control display during the match.

The description mentions 88 player nations, but Roblox's separate Server Size field showed 30 when a third-party guide checked. These are different fields, and the listing does not explain how they relate.
