RoFront [World PvP] is a real-time territory conquest game on Roblox by Torrey Pines. According to its official description, it is based on the Open Front game, and the map is credited to Open Front. You begin with a single nation, expand outward, attack neighbors, build cities, ports, and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes while competing against other nations and tribes. The advertised victory condition is to conquer 80% of the world.

This RoFront beginner guide focuses on what the official listing actually documents, what a third-party guide observed, and what remains unverified. It is not a soldier shooter, so there are no classes, rifles, or kill/death ratios to learn here. The core loop is map-based expansion and control.

## What RoFront Actually Is

The official Roblox page describes RoFront as "real-time territory conquest." That phrase matters. You are not managing a single character; you are managing a nation's position on a world map. The description says you expand from one nation, attack neighbors, and build infrastructure. It also says you can forge alliances and launch nukes.

The description mentions "88 player nations and hundreds of tribes." Treat that as an advertised feature claim, not a confirmed server capacity. A third-party guide from Nisu noted that Roblox's separate Server Size field showed 30 when checked on October 11, 2026, and explained that the listing does not clarify how the two numbers relate. For your first session, use the current server information shown on the Roblox page rather than assuming a specific player count.

You can find the official experience here: [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront). Always confirm the creator is Torrey Pines, since similar names can lead to unrelated games.

## Reading Your Starting Position

A territory conquest game rewards information before action. Before you attack anyone, locate your own territory, identify which nations share your borders, and find the world-control display. The Nisu guide suggests watching your borders and the world-control meter before committing to an attack, rather than judging progress only by how large your colored area looks at the current zoom.

This is sound advice inferred from the genre, not a measured mechanic. In any map-based conquest game, your neighbors are your immediate threats and opportunities. A border shared with a large nation is different from a border shared with a small one. The official description does not document specific troop types, damage values, or combat formulas, so any claim about exact attack strength would be invented.

A practical first-match routine: make one deliberate move, watch what changes, then decide again. Did you gain territory? Did another border shift? Did your resources change? This gives you a reference point without relying on an unverified build order.

## Alliances and Nukes

Alliances and nuclear attacks are part of the published feature set. The description says you can forge alliances and launch nukes. It does not explain how alliances are formed, whether they are binding, or what a nuke costs.

Because these actions can change relationships with other nations, read the relevant in-game controls before acting. A dramatic nuclear strike in a video does not prove that rushing toward nukes is the best opening. The Nisu guide makes a similar point: a dramatic action in a recording does not establish an optimal strategy for your position.

## The 80% Goal and How to Think About Winning

The official description says to conquer 80% of the world to win. Keep that goal visible. A successful local attack can improve your position, but it does not complete the match by itself. Follow the world-control display rather than assuming one large territory has won the game.

This guide does not promise you will win. Territory conquest depends on your neighbors, your decisions, and how other nations act. The useful beginner habit is to review the move that changed your position most and ask what you would do differently.
