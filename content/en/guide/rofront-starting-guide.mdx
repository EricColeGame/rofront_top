RoFront [World PvP] is a real-time territory conquest game on Roblox from the creator Torrey Pines. If you are searching for a RoFront starting guide, the first thing to understand is what kind of game this is: you are not controlling a single soldier in a first-person shooter. You are managing a nation on a map, expanding your borders, and competing against other nations for control of the world. The official description says the game is based on Open Front, credits Open Front for the map, and sets a clear victory condition: conquer 80% of the world to win.

That single sentence shapes almost every early decision. You can find the experience here: [RoFront [World PvP] on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront). Before you join, confirm the creator name is Torrey Pines, because similarly named games and videos can lead you somewhere unrelated.

## What RoFront Actually Is

The official Roblox description lists the core loop: expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports, and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes. It also mentions 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes. Treat that as an advertised feature of the description, not as a confirmed server capacity. Roblox lists server size separately, and the two numbers do not necessarily mean the same thing. When you join, check the current server information in the Roblox client rather than assuming a specific player count.

Because RoFront is map-based and real-time, your attention should go to territory, borders, and the world-control display. A tutorial for a Roblox shooter will not teach you this game. The skills that matter here are reading the map, choosing when to expand, and understanding what each building or diplomatic action is supposed to do for your position.

## Read Your Position Before You Move

For a first match, resist the urge to attack immediately. Take a moment to locate your territory, identify which nations share your borders, and find the world-control meter. That meter matters more than how large your colored area looks at your current zoom level. A big-looking blob on one part of the map can still be far from the 80% goal.

A useful beginner routine, suggested as strategy rather than measured mechanics, is to make one deliberate move and then watch what changes. Did you gain territory? Did another border shift? Did your nation's displayed information change? This gives you a reference point for your next decision without relying on an unverified opening build order. If a push goes poorly, ask which decision you would change: the target, the timing, or a threat you ignored.

## Expansion, Buildings, and Alliances

The official description names cities, ports, and factories as things you can build, and alliances and nukes as actions you can take. It does not publish a complete economy table, so no guide can honestly tell you the single cheapest or best build order. The practical approach is to inspect each option in-game, read its displayed cost and stated benefit, and ask what you are trying to improve with that choice.

| Advertised feature | What to check in your match |
| --- | --- |
| Territory expansion | Your borders, your target, and world-control progress |
| Cities, ports, factories | Current description, cost, and intended benefit |
| Alliances | Who is involved and what the current controls offer |
| Nuclear attacks | Requirements and the consequences of the action |

Treat each of these as a separate system to learn rather than copying whatever a video shows first. Alliances and nukes can change your relationships with other nations, so read the relevant in-game controls before committing. A dramatic nuclear strike in a clip does not prove that rushing toward nukes is the right opening for your position.

## Using Video Footage Without Being Misled

Third-party footage can help you learn the interface. One example is a [third-party guide from Nisu](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/) that references an APEX ROBLOX match video and notes the world-control goal at the top of the screen, colored borders across the map, and troop, gold, and attack controls below. That guide is an independent observation, not developer documentation, and it explicitly says the author had not played a live match.

If you watch footage, pause on the interface and match one visible control to the action that follows, then resume to see the result. Be careful with playback speed: a video shown at 2× speed is not a real-time measurement of income or attack speed. Also remember that a single match reflects that player's resources, neighbors, and territory, not a guaranteed strategy for yours.

## FAQ

**How do you win RoFront?**
The official Torrey Pines description says to conquer 80% of the world. Follow the world-control display in your match rather than assuming one large territory has already won.

**Does RoFront support 88 players in one server?**
The description mentions 88 player nations. That is an advertised figure, not a confirmed simultaneous server capacity. Check the current Roblox server information when you join.

**What should I build first?**
Inspect the current city, port, and factory options and choose based on your position and their displayed requirements. The official listing does not establish a universal optimal order.

**Is RoFront a shooter?**
No. It is a real-time territory conquest game. Focus on borders, expansion, buildings, alliances, and the 80% goal.

Start small: recognize your position, make one measured expansion, inspect one building, and watch the control meter. That is a more reliable first session than chasing an unverified build order.
