RoFront [World PvP] is a real-time territory conquest game on Roblox from the creator Torrey Pines. According to the official game page, it is based on Open Front, and the map is credited to Open Front as well. You start with a single nation, expand outward, attack neighbors, build cities, ports, and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes while competing against other nations and tribes. The advertised victory condition is simple to state and hard to reach: conquer 80% of the world.

This RoFront strategy guide focuses on decisions the official description actually supports. It separates what Torrey Pines advertises from what a territory conquest premise suggests you should try, and it flags the parts of the game that are not documented anywhere yet.

## Start by reading the board, not by attacking

The official description lists attacking neighbors as part of the core loop, but it does not tell you when to attack or how much it costs. That means your first useful habit is observation, not aggression.

When a match begins, find your own territory and trace its borders. Identify which nations and tribes touch you, because those are the only neighbors you can attack directly at the start. Then look for the world-control display. A third-party guide from Nisu, which describes itself as an independent, unplayed observation of the interface, notes that the world-control goal appears at the top of the screen and that colored borders mark territory across the map. Treat that as a secondary observation rather than developer documentation, but it is a reasonable place to start looking.

The practical takeaway: judge your progress by the world-control meter, not by how large your colored area looks at your current zoom level. A big-looking blob can still be a small share of the world.

## Expansion advice inferred from a conquest premise

Everything in this section is strategy reasoning, not measured mechanics. The official description does not publish attack formulas, troop counts, or costs, so no guide can honestly give you exact numbers.

With that caveat, a territory conquest game usually rewards a few habits:

- Expand in one direction at a time. Splitting your attention across multiple borders makes it harder to tell which move actually helped.
- Watch your other borders while you push. The Nisu guide suggests asking whether you overlooked a neighboring threat when a push goes poorly, which is sound advice for any map-based conquest game.
- Make one deliberate move, then watch what changes. Note the territory gained, the information shown for your nation, and any shift along another border before repeating.
- Do not assume one big attack wins. The stated goal is 80% of the world, so a successful local push improves your position without ending the match.

If a push fails, treat it as information. Was the target poorly chosen, did you commit while a neighbor was exposed, or did you act without understanding the current controls? Those questions are more useful than copying a number from someone else's match.

## Cities, ports, and factories: what the description does and does not say

Torrey Pines advertises cities, ports, and factories as buildable structures. That is the full extent of the verified information. The official listing does not include an economy table, building costs, or a recommended build order, so any guide claiming a universal "cheapest route to victory" is inventing it.

| Advertised feature | What to check in your own match |
| --- | --- |
| Cities, ports, factories | The current description, displayed cost, and intended benefit of each option |
| Alliances | Who is involved and what the current in-game controls offer |
| Nuclear attacks | The requirements and the consequences of the selected action |
| Territory expansion | Your borders, your target, and world-control progress |

A useful first-session question is: what am I trying to improve with this choice? Read the information shown for the building, compare it with your present situation, then watch the result. Treat each structure as a separate option to learn rather than buying whichever icon a video shows first.

## Alliances and nukes are advertised, not explained

The official description names alliances and nuclear attacks as features. It does not explain how alliances are formed, what they obligate you to do, or what a nuke costs or destroys. Because an action that changes relationships with other nations can backfire, read the relevant in-game controls before committing, especially for anything that looks irreversible.

A dramatic nuclear strike in a gameplay video does not establish that rushing toward nukes is the best opening for your position. It shows one player's choice in one match.

## The 88-nation claim and server size are different things

The description refers to 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes. That is a claim about the game's design, not a promise that any single server holds 88 simultaneous players. On the official Roblox page, the separate Server Size field showed 30 when the Nisu guide checked it on October 11, 2026. Those are different fields, and the listing does not explain how the two counts relate.

Use the current Roblox server information when choosing where to join. Do not treat 88 as a tested player capacity, and do not treat a crowded recording as proof of how the game will perform on your device.

## A first-session routine

If you want a concrete plan for your first match, try this sequence, all of it inferred from the conquest premise rather than from published mechanics:

1. Locate your territory and its borders.
2. Identify your immediate neighbors.
3. Find the world-control display and note your current share.
4. Make one measured expansion and watch what changes.
5. Inspect one building option and read its displayed cost and benefit.
6. Check the alliance and nuclear controls without necessarily using them.
7. After the match, review the single move that changed your position the most.

For interface learning, match footage can help. The Nisu guide references an APEX ROBLOX video from October 10, 2026 that follows a match from a small territory to a larger one, with the world-control goal at the top and troop, gold, and attack controls below. Note that the sampled segment runs at 2× speed, so do not use its playback timing as a real-time income or attack-speed measurement.

## FAQ

**How do you win RoFront?**
The official Torrey Pines description says to conquer 80% of the world. Follow the world-control display in the match rather than assuming one large territory has won the game.

**Does RoFront support 88 players in one server?**
The description mentions 88 player nations. Roblox's separate Server Size field showed 30 when a third-party guide checked it. These are different figures, and live capacity has not been independently tested here.

**What should I build first?**
Inspect the current city, port, and factory options and choose according to your position and their displayed requirements. The official listing does not establish a universal optimal buying order.

**Where can I verify controls, codes, or updates?**
Only through the actual experience. Open [RoFront [World PvP] on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront), confirm the creator is Torrey Pines, and read the in-game controls directly. No codes, hotkeys, chat commands, custom map tools, Discord links, or patch notes are documented in the sources reviewed for this guide, so do not trust any page that claims otherwise without showing you where it came from.

**Is RoFront a shooter?**
No. It is a map-based real-time territory conquest game. Advice written for Roblox shooters will not explain this experience.
