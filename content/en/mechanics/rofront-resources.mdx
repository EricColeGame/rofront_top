## Core Types of RoFront Resources and How They Work

In frontline tactical combat, logistics are broken down into distinct categories to simulate operational supply lines. Instead of relying solely on isolated soldier loadouts, squads must monitor team-wide and personal assets. Understanding each commodity allows teams to prioritize objectives during chaotic pushes.

The primary assets you manage revolve around structural components, munition stockpiles, requisition points, and operational reinforcement tickets. While raw combatants often focus on weapon reload speeds and sightlines, logistics coordinators track the flow of these materials across dynamic capture points.

| Resource Category | Acquisition Method | Primary Battlefield Purpose | Strategic Impact Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Munitions & Ammo** | Supply crates, support drops, depot resupply | Replenishing magazines, heavy ordinances, and explosives | High (Immediate survival) |

### Munitions and Supply Caches
Munitions dictate how long an infantry unit can sustain continuous pressure on an enemy position. Running dry during an assault forces squads to pull back or switch to sidearms, leaving them vulnerable to counter-charges. Forward ammunition crates provide a steady stream of bullets, grenades, and mortar shells, turning a fragile outpost into an impenetrable bastion.

### Construction Materials and Scrap
Combat engineering relies on salvage and scrap gathered near active points or delivered from rear-echelon hubs. Engineers and support personnel convert construction scrap into vital structures such as reinforced sandbag walls, sniper perches, and barricades. Protecting these materials ensures that captured ground can be locked down before the opposing team organizes a counter-offensive.

## Tactical Allocation: Fortification vs. Firepower

Once accumulated, knowing where to spend your squad's assets determines whether you hold an objective or waste valuable supplies on fragile barricades. The tension between buying heavy defensive structures and calling in offensive ordnance defines tactical depth.

Allocating **RoFront resources** must match the current phase of the engagement. Spending everything on offensive barrages leaves your squad with zero defensive cover if the strike fails to clear the point completely.

| Match Situation | Recommended Resource Focus | Priority Deployables | Common Pitfall to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Initial Objective Rush** | Mobility & Rapid Cover | Light sandbags, forward ammo crates | Over-investing in static turrets early |
| **Mid-Match Trench Siege** | Heavy Fortification & Healing | Heavy barricades, medical stations, resupply | Neglecting rear flank security |
| **Breaking Defensive Stalemates** | Ordnance & Heavy Weaponry | Mortars, artillery strikes, smoke barrages | Firing blind without squad spotters |
| **Final Sector Defense** | Chokepoint Reinforcement | Barbed wire, layered sandbags, ammo hubs | Clumping all supplies in a single trench |

### Prioritizing Chokepoint Fortifications
When building defenses, avoid scattering barricades across open fields. Focus construction on narrow bottlenecks where enemy troops are forced to funnel through. Placing barbed wire to slow enemy sprint speeds alongside a protected sandbag revetment gives your defenders clean sightlines and ample cover against incoming small arms fire.

### Balancing Munition Supply with Heavy Fire Support
Deploying expensive artillery or mortar emplacements requires steady munition consumption. Squads must ensure they have sustained ammunition reserves before dropping heavy hardware, or the emplacement will sit idle after firing a single salvo.

## Squad Roles and Supply Line Management

Efficient logistics rely on role division within the squad. When every player tries to play frontline rifleman, nobody builds resupply points or repairs defensive structures. Establishing defined responsibilities ensures that **RoFront resources** are collected and utilized smoothly.

Player experience shows that squads featuring at least one dedicated combat engineer and one support logistics specialist maintain defensive positions more than twice as long as squads without dedicated roles.

| Squad Role | Logistics Responsibility | Primary Tool / Equipment | Key Objective in Resource Economy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Combat Engineer** | Structure erection and fortification repair | Building hammer, repair kit | Constructing chokepoints and maintaining forward cover |
| **Support Logistics** | Munition replenishment and distribution | Ammo packs, supply transport | Keeping gunners stocked and ferrying field crates |
| **Assault Leader** | Directing resource spending and tactical strikes | Field radio, binoculars | Designating priority targets for high-cost ordnance |
| **Scout / Spotter** | Locating enemy supply caches and spotting flanks | Recon binoculars, smoke | Identifying targets and scouting safe gathering paths |

### The Role of the Combat Engineer
Engineers transform raw battlefield scrap into structural defense. By constantly repairing damaged sandbags under fire, engineers deny attackers the chance to breach defenses. They also disassemble obsolete or poorly placed structures, refunding a fraction of the scrap for reuse elsewhere on the map.

### The Support Specialist as Field Courier
Support specialists bridge the gap between backline bases and forward trenches. By carrying modular ammo pouches and toolkits, they keep combatants armed directly on the firing line, eliminating the downtime caused when riflemen run back to distant depots for magazines.

## Common Resource Mistakes to Avoid

Even seasoned players fall into bad logistics habits that cost their teams victory. Avoiding these fundamental errors will instantly elevate your match win rate and make you a prized squad teammate.

* **Hoarding Requisition Points:** Saving tactical points for a match-ending super-weapon that you never end up using is a massive waste. Regular, medium-tier support strikes often turn the tide far more reliably.
* **Over-Building Abandoned Sectors:** Expending all your team's scrap on a capture point that the enemy has already bypassed leaves forward frontline units completely exposed.
* **Ignoring Depot Defense:** Forgetting to guard your team's rear resupply depots allows enemy flankers to sever your supply lines, starving frontline soldiers of munitions.
* **Clumping Supply Caches:** Placing all ammo boxes in one bunker creates a high-value target for enemy mortars and grenades. Distribute your crates across multiple dugouts instead.

Player reports demonstrate that spread-out logistics hubs survive sustained artillery bombardments far better than concentrated, single-point depots. Keep your supply chain flexible, responsive, and well-camouflaged.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the fastest ways to replenish depleted RoFront resources during a match?
The quickest method is capturing secondary objective hubs and escorting supply crates directly from your team's base depot. Teaming up with a combat engineer and a support specialist allows you to collect neutral scrap clusters rapidly under smoke cover, restoring frontline stockpiles in minutes.

### Do RoFront resources persist between different combat rounds?
In standard match rotations, all unspent materials, tactical points, and physical fortifications reset when a new round starts. You should spend your operational assets during decisive combat pushes rather than stockpiling them until the final whistle.

### How do support classes help distribute vital RoFront resources to squad mates?
Support classes carry specialized supply packs and portable ammo crates that can be placed directly inside forward trenches. This allows assault troops to restock magazines and bandages right on the front lines without having to fall back to rear bases.

### Can enemy squads capture or steal our team's stockpiled RoFront resources?
Yes. If an enemy overrun takes a forward outpost holding unspent supply crates or scrap depots, they can either capture the stockpile for their own war effort or destroy it to cut off your local supply line. Always guard storage points or booby-trap them when an organized retreat becomes necessary.
