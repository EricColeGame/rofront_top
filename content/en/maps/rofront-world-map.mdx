The RoFront world map is the center of everything in RoFront [World PvP], the real-time territory conquest game by Torrey Pines on Roblox. You do not spawn into a shooter match with a rifle and a squad. You spawn as a single nation on a shared world map, surrounded by neighbors, and the entire match is about expanding your colored territory until someone controls the map. If you are searching for the RoFront world map, the most useful thing to understand is that the map is not a static image you download or edit. It is the live board where every player's decisions play out.

## What the RoFront world map actually is

According to the official Roblox listing, RoFront is "based on the Open Front game" and is a "real-time territory conquest" experience. The same description credits Open Front for the map itself. That credit matters: it tells you the world map is inherited from another game's design rather than built as a custom RoFront map editor or a set of player-made maps.

The official description also states the core loop: expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes. The advertised victory condition is to conquer 80% of the world. So the RoFront world map is both your starting position and your win condition. You are not trying to eliminate one opponent or hold one objective. You are trying to paint enough of the map your color.

One important clarification: the description mentions "88 player nations and hundreds of tribes." That is a description of the game's scope, not a confirmed server capacity. Roblox lists server size as a separate field, and a third-party guide from Nisu noted that field showed 30 when checked. Treat 88 as an advertised figure about nations in the game world, not a promise that 88 humans share your server.

## Reading the map before you act

The most practical skill on the RoFront world map is reading your position. Before you attack anyone, identify three things:

- Where your territory sits and which nations share your borders
- How large your colored area looks at the current zoom
- What the world-control display shows

That last point is easy to overlook. A third-party guide from Nisu recommends watching the world-control meter rather than judging progress only by how big your territory appears on screen. Zoom level can make a small nation look large and a large nation look modest. The meter is the more honest measure of progress toward the advertised 80% goal.

This is strategic advice inferred from a territory conquest premise, not a measured mechanic. The official listing does not publish exact meter behavior, so verify it in your own match.

## Borders, neighbors, and the expansion decision

In a territory conquest game, your border is your front line. The official description says you attack your neighbors, which means the nations touching your territory are the ones you can realistically pressure first. A neighbor who is already fighting someone else on another border may be a softer target than one who is idle.

A reasonable first-match routine, based on the premise rather than any published formula:

1. Locate your territory and note every shared border.
2. Make one deliberate expansion move.
3. Watch what changes: territory gained, your nation's displayed information, and any movement along another border.
4. Repeat with that reference in mind.

If a push goes badly, the useful question is not "what percentage always wins" but "which decision would I change?" Was the target poorly chosen, did you ignore a neighboring threat, or did you commit without understanding the current controls?

A dramatic nuclear strike in a video does not prove that rushing toward nukes is the best opening for your position.
