The RoFront map is the whole game board. In [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) by Torrey Pines, you do not spawn into a corridor or a lobby — you spawn into a nation on a world map, and everything you do happens by changing what that map looks like. The official description calls it "real-time territory conquest," based on the Open Front game, with map credits going to Open Front. That single sentence explains why the map matters more here than in almost any other Roblox experience: your territory, your borders, and your neighbors are the actual state of the match.

This guide separates what the developer advertises from what players can reasonably infer, and it flags what is simply not documented yet.

## What the RoFront map actually is

According to the official Roblox listing, RoFront is a real-time territory conquest game. You "expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports and factories, forge alliances and launch nukes against 88 player nations and hundreds of tribes." The stated victory condition is to "conquer 80% of the world."

That is the entire verified feature set. Note what it does not say. There is no mention of troop types, unit stats, damage numbers, resource formulas, hotkeys, or build orders. The description names systems — expansion, cities, ports, factories, alliances, nukes — but not their implementation. Anyone telling you the exact cost of a factory or the optimal opening attack is working from their own match, not from developer documentation.

The map itself is credited to Open Front. That means the geography you are looking at is borrowed from another game's design, not custom-built for RoFront. Open Front's own rules, however, cannot tell you how RoFront's controls or balance work. Treat the map layout as shared heritage and the mechanics as RoFront's own.

## Reading your position before you move

The most useful thing a new player can do on the RoFront map is nothing at all for the first minute. Locate your territory. Identify which nations share your border. Find the world-control display, which the official description implies through its 80% win condition.

A third-party guide from [Nisu](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/) makes a reasonable point here: judge your progress by the world-control meter, not by how large your colored area looks at your current zoom level. Zoomed in, a modest empire can look enormous. Zoomed out, it can look like a speck. The meter is the honest number.

This is advice inferred from a territory conquest premise, not a measured mechanic. In any game where the win condition is a percentage of the world, the percentage is the scoreboard.

## Borders, neighbors, and the cost of a bad push

Because RoFront is map-based, every action you take redraws a boundary somewhere. That has two consequences worth internalizing.

First, attacking a neighbor changes your border with that neighbor. If the push succeeds, your frontier moves. If it stalls, you have spent whatever the attack cost and gained nothing — and you may have revealed your intentions to someone who now has a reason to look at you.

Second, expanding in one direction usually means leaving another direction thinner. The official description mentions "hundreds of tribes" alongside the 88 player nations, which suggests the map is not empty space between players. What those tribes do, how they behave, and whether they can be negotiated with is not documented in the listing. Verify it in an actual match rather than assuming.

## Buildings, alliances, and nukes: learn them as separate systems

The official listing groups cities, ports, and factories together as things you build, but it does not describe what any of them do. A port and a factory are presumably not interchangeable — otherwise there would be no reason to name both — but the description stops short of explaining the difference.

The practical approach is to inspect each option in-game, read whatever the interface shows you, and ask one question before committing: what am I trying to improve right now? If you are boxed in by neighbors, a building that helps you project force matters more than one that helps you consolidate. If you have room to grow, the reverse may be true. That reasoning comes from the conquest premise, not from a verified economy table.

Alliances and nukes deserve extra caution because both change your relationships with other nations. The description confirms they exist. It does not confirm when they become available, what they cost, or what happens diplomatically after you use them. Read the in-game controls before acting, especially for anything irreversible.

## The 88-nation claim and server size

The official description says you compete against "88 player nations." That is a description of the game's scope, not a promise about server capacity. Roblox lists server size as a separate field, and the two numbers should not be treated as the same thing. When you join, check the current server information on the Roblox page rather than assuming a specific player count.

## FAQ

**How do you win on the RoFront map?**
The official description says to conquer 80% of the world. Watch the world-control display rather than assuming one big territory has sealed the match.

**Is the RoFront map the same as Open Front's?**
The description credits Open Front for the map. That does not mean the two games share rules, controls, or balance.

**What should I build first?**
Not documented. Inspect the city, port, and factory options in your match and choose based on your position and their displayed requirements.

**Where do I verify anything not covered here?**
Open [RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) directly, confirm the creator is Torrey Pines, and read the in-game interface. Searches for similar names can surface unrelated games, including shooters that share none of these systems.
