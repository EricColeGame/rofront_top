## What RoFront Actually Is

[RoFront [World PvP]](https://www.roblox.com/games/137151641329864/RoFront) is a Roblox strategy experience by the creator **Torrey Pines**. The official description states it is "based on the Open Front game" and credits Open Front for the map. It describes itself as **real-time territory conquest**.

That framing matters. RoFront is not a soldier-level shooter. There are no classes, loadouts, or kill/death tracking in the official description. It is a map-based game about nations, borders, and control of territory. If you search for RoFront and land on a tutorial for a Roblox shooter with a similar name, you are in the wrong place.

The advertised loop, per the description, is: expand from a single nation, attack your neighbors, build cities, ports, and factories, forge alliances, and launch nukes. The stated victory condition is to **conquer 80% of the world**.

This is where most "news" searches go wrong. The description names features. It does not explain how they work.

| Alliances | That they can be forged; no terms or mechanics |
| Nukes | That they can be launched; no requirements or effects |
| 88 player nations, hundreds of tribes | A description claim, not a confirmed server capacity |

Notice the gap. "Build cities" tells you the option exists. It does not tell you what a city does, what it costs, or whether it is worth building first. Anyone who gives you exact numbers for these systems is either reading them from inside a live match or making them up. The official listing does not provide an economy table, so no external article can honestly claim a universal optimal build order.

## The 88 Nations Claim and Server Size

The description mentions "88 player nations and hundreds of tribes." That is worth reading carefully. It is a statement about the game's design scope, not a promise that any given server holds 88 simultaneous players.

A third-party guide from [Nisu](https://nisu.io/guides/rofront-roblox-guide/) noted that Roblox's separate Server Size field displayed 30 when checked, and correctly pointed out that these are different fields. The listing alone does not explain how the two relate. So the honest position is: treat 88 as an advertised figure, and check the current server information on the Roblox page when you decide where to join.

## Practical Strategy, Clearly Labeled as Inference

Everything below is reasoning from the territory conquest premise. It is not measured mechanics, and it is not a guaranteed path to winning.

**Read your position before you move.** Locate your territory, its borders, and your neighbors. In any border-based conquest game, the nation sharing your boundary is the one that matters most in the opening minutes.

**Watch the world-control meter, not just your colored area.** The published goal is 80% of the world. A large-looking blob at your current zoom level can be misleading. The meter is the number tied to the actual win condition.

**Make one deliberate move and observe.** Expand once, then watch what changes: territory gained, information shown for your nation, and any shift along another border. That gives you a reference point for the next decision without relying on an unverified opening build order.

**Treat buildings as separate questions.** Cities, ports, and factories are named as buildable, but the description does not rank them. Ask what you are trying to improve with a given choice, read the in-game information shown for it, and watch the result.

**Be careful with alliances and nukes.** Both are advertised features that can change your relationships with other nations. Read the relevant in-game controls before acting. A dramatic nuclear strike in a video does not establish that rushing toward one is the right opening for your position.

## How to Verify RoFront News Yourself

Because there is no documented update feed, the reliable sources are narrow:

1. **The official Roblox page.** Check the description, the creator name (Torrey Pines), and the current server information. If the description changes, that is real news.
2. **The creator's profile.** Torrey Pines is the authority on this experience. Anything not from the creator or the experience page is secondary.
3. **Live match footage, watched critically.** Player videos show the interface as it existed when recorded. Watch where the player looks before each action and match a visible control to the result. Do not treat playback speed as a real-time measurement of income or attack speed.

What you should not trust: invented patch notes, fake codes, unverified Discord links, or any article quoting exact troop counts, damage values, or build timings. None of that appears in the verified sources.
